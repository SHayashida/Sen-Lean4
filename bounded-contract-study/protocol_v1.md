## 1. Question and claim boundary

**RQ:** In a pre-specified sample of software-repair pull requests, do
review-time interactions reveal downstream obligations that were not explicit
in the permitted pre-review evidence, and, when this occurs, does the
subsequent repair materially change strategy?

This is a separate empirical software-engineering study of public records.
It is designed to permit a null or negative result. Its unit of sampling is a
PR; its unit of obligation coding is an individually identified acceptance
constraint within that PR. Multiple comments repeating one constraint do not
constitute multiple independent cases.

| Level | Question or claim | What this protocol licenses |
|---|---|---|
| O1: occurrence | An acceptance obligation first becomes explicit during review, after a public candidate already exists. | Potentially supportable in the sampled, reconstructable evidence frame only. |
| O2: consequence | An O1 obligation is connected by evidence to a material change of repair strategy. | Potentially supportable as a documented revision sequence; not an estimate of a counterfactual causal effect. |
| F1: controlled mechanism | Under bounded search, generation-time pre-binding to one contract can exclude candidates valid under another. | Requires a separate controlled experiment with defined budgets, candidate universes, contracts, and comparisons; not tested here. |
| F2: production causation | Such pre-binding actually caused candidate loss in an observed production workflow. | **NOT CLAIMED.** Requires future direct evidence of generation constraints, omitted alternatives, their validity, and the causal connection. |

**O1 + O2 do not logically imply F1 or F2.** A review sequence neither exposes
an unobserved search frontier nor establishes that an alternative was excluded
by pre-binding. No claim is made that requirements always emerge late,
pre-binding is generally harmful, APR necessarily loses valid repairs, or
review histories establish general M3 validity.

The narrow positive report would be: “Within this specified sample and
recoverable public record, we observed review-time articulation of previously
non-explicit acceptance obligations; some were linked to material revisions.”
Report actual counts and uncertainty, including zero. “First explicit” means
first demonstrable in the bounded evidence corpus, not first known to any
participant or first required by the software's semantics.

## 3. Construct: downstream contract obligation

For this study, a downstream contract obligation is a testable or inspectable
constraint on acceptable repair behavior or assurance **beyond compilation or
the immediately targeted local repair condition**. “Downstream” includes
callers, consumers, other components, versions, executions, resources, or
operational responsibilities; it need not mean a literal call-graph edge.
This operational use of “contract” does not assert a formal M3 contract.

A coder must record all of the following for each proposed obligation:

1. A normalized proposition: under condition X, repaired behavior or assurance
   must satisfy Y for the named consumer, execution class, or boundary Z.
2. A concrete consequence of violation, beyond taste or administrative work.
3. A source excerpt or artifact span supporting the proposition and its time.
4. Why it is additional to, rather than a restatement of, the apparent local
   repair objective frozen in Phase A.

If the proposition, consequence, or additional scope cannot be supported, code
`contract_qualification = ambiguous` or `no`, with reasons. Do not manufacture
an unstated downstream purpose for a reviewer preference. A previously
unmentioned ordinary correction of the original local bug is not automatically
a new downstream obligation.

Permitted multi-label categories are:

| Category | Required semantic content |
|---|---|
| `api_behavior` | Public inputs, outputs, or behavior promised to consumers. |
| `version_upgrade` | Backward compatibility or mixed-version/rolling-upgrade behavior. |
| `security` | Confidentiality, integrity, authorization, or an explicit threat boundary. |
| `resource_performance` | A resource/work/time bound or required operational performance property. |
| `distributed_boundary` | Behavior across nodes, partitions, replication, or failure boundaries. |
| `error_semantics` | Observable failure type, propagation, recovery, or error contract. |
| `persistence_serialization` | Stored or wire representation and compatibility/integrity requirements. |
| `caller_callee` | Preconditions, postconditions, or propagated obligations between components. |
| `lifecycle_cleanup` | Ownership, release, cancellation, or lifetime requirements. |
| `concurrency` | Ordering, synchronization, atomicity, or concurrent-execution constraints. |
| `operational_observability` | Required externally usable operational signals or diagnostic behavior. |
| `cross_component_invariant` | An invariant requiring coordinated behavior across components. |
| `other_semantic` | A written proposition meeting all four tests but fitting no listed category. |

Formatting, naming taste, style-only refactoring, prose wording, changelog
administration, trivial cleanup, and test renaming are excluded unless an
explicit behavioral or assurance consequence satisfies the same four tests.
A test request is not itself proof of a new obligation: identify the semantic
property tested. A log-message request can be semantic if, for example, a
specified consumer relies on that signal; a preferred wording alone is not.

Record the obligation's review disposition separately as `accepted`,
`disputed`, `retracted`, or `unresolved`. Accepted requires explicit author
acknowledgment, or an expressly linked implementation/test change implementing
the requirement. Silence, merging the PR, or a bot's assertion alone is not
acceptance. Disputed/retracted/unresolved requests remain visible, but cannot
be primary O1 positives. This deliberately studies accepted obligations and
may undercount legitimate but unresolved requests.

## 4. Fixed frame and deterministic sample

### 4.1 Population, frames, and exclusions

The target population is public repair PRs in `elastic/elasticsearch` merged
in **[2026-04-01T00:00:00Z, 2026-07-01T00:00:00Z)**. This is the most recent
complete calendar quarter whose end precedes the protocol date by at least
30 days, permitting equal post-merge observation. The dates are now fixed;
do not move the window because it yields few or uninteresting cases.

Distinguish and publish the counts of:

- **Population:** all repair PRs in that project/window, including ones whose
  eligibility cannot be determined from public evidence.
- **Candidate frame:** every publicly enumerable merged PR in that window,
  before repair or review screening; no outcome-oriented keyword filter.
- **Excluded known-positive cases:** the IDs and explicit derivatives in
  Section 2, recorded even if outside the candidate frame.
- **Eligible frame:** candidate PRs satisfying every rule below with sufficient
  screening evidence. Its conditional nature limits all subsequent claims.
- **Sample:** up to 100 PRs selected from the frozen eligible frame by the
  fixed hash ranking below. It is not a hand-picked collection of examples.

Eligibility requires:

1. A pre-review title/body, linked issue, code, or test establishes correction
   of existing incorrect behavior, regression, or violated invariant. Record
   the concrete defect and pointer. Feature additions, pure optimization
   without a stated violated requirement, and pure refactoring are excluded.
   A current `bug` label alone is insufficient.
2. The pre-review candidate changes executable production behavior, including
   runtime configuration interpreted by the shipped system. Test-, docs-,
   example-, benchmark-, and developer-build-only changes are excluded.
   File location alone is insufficient: record why the affected code ships or
   runs as part of the product.
3. It is an original repair PR. Explicit backports, cherry-picks, duplicates,
   and dependency/version-bump-only updates are excluded. Mixed changes remain
   eligible only if a distinct production defect and its code repair are
   supported before review; do not use later review outcomes to decide this.
4. The PR is not opened by an account explicitly identified as a bot or
   automation. Unknown author type is recorded and retained; no identity
   inference from a name alone. Human-opened AI-assisted PRs are not excluded.
5. At least one non-author candidate-evaluating review interaction exists.
   Approval-only evaluation is enough; a request for change is **not** required.
   Human and automated review both qualify. Scheduling, label changes,
   assignment, and merge administration do not. CI status alone does not.

Do not require a new obligation, disagreement, many comments, code changes
after review, strategy change, or a positive outcome for eligibility.
No-review PRs are counted as exclusions, not coded as negative O1 cases.
This study therefore cannot estimate rates across all repair PRs.

For unresolved repair/production/backport/review eligibility,
use `screening_unknown`, retain it in the candidate-frame ledger, and exclude
it from the eligible frame with the specific missing evidence. An unknown author
account type alone remains eligible under rule 4; evidence conflicting about
automation is a screening ambiguity. Do not silently
classify uncertainty as non-repair. Report screening attrition; the eligible
frame is not a complete census of the population. Availability of every
historical field is not an additional eligibility rule: selected cases with
incomplete temporal evidence remain sampled unknowns.

### 4.2 Enumeration, screening, and ordering

A data custodian who will not serve as a blinded outcome coder performs the
following only **after the protocol freeze**:

1. Enumerate all closed PRs through the repository API with complete
   pagination in creation-time order; filter on immutable PR identity and
   `merged_at` in the fixed window. Do not use search ranking, “interesting”
   labels, contract keywords,
   or a first-page/top-k cap. Partition retrieval if API limits require it,
   and reconcile page coverage and duplicate IDs. Repeat the enumeration until
   two consecutive passes yield the same filtered identity set; retain both
   receipts and all retrieval failures. Record exact requests,
   retrieval times, response hashes, tool versions, and failures.
2. Freeze the candidate list and metadata snapshot before screening. Changed
   titles/labels are retrieval-time metadata, not historical pre-review facts.
   If complete enumeration cannot be demonstrated, stop and report frame
   failure; do not substitute convenient PRs.
3. Two screeners independently apply the eligibility rules using pre-review
   material plus only metadata needed to establish merge state, review
   existence, author type, and explicit duplicate/backport relations. Review
   outcomes must not influence screening. The custodian may necessarily see
   review text while reconstructing timing; log this exposure and keep that
   person out of blinded coding. A third screener resolves rule application;
   irreducible uncertainty remains `screening_unknown`.
4. Commit and hash the full screening ledger, including both initial decisions
   and adjudication. Freeze the eligible list before sampling.
5. For each eligible PR, compute SHA-256 over the UTF-8 bytes of
   `BCS-O1O2-v1\nelastic/elasticsearch#<decimal PR number>\n`, where `\n`
   means an actual LF byte, and the number has no leading zeros. Sort by the
   lowercase 64-character digest ascending, then PR number ascending for a
   collision. Take the first `n = min(100, N_eligible)` entries.
6. Freeze the sample IDs, ranks, list hash, and screening exclusions in a
   committed run manifest before any Phase A coding. Keep all selected slots.

The fixed cap of 100 is a pragmatic initial-study workload choice for full
independent double coding, **not** a power calculation or a statistically
justified precision target. No occurrence estimate is available to justify
one. Do not expand, shrink, rerank, or stop the sample after seeing outcomes.
If fewer than 100 are eligible, use the complete eligible frame. If none are
eligible, report an empty-frame feasibility result. If resources cannot support
the fixed plan, stop before coding and publish a new protocol version; do not
choose a favorable smaller sample.

This public hash rule is a reproducible selection convention, not proof of
probability sampling. Primary analysis is descriptive; no population prevalence
inference, confidence-interval coverage claim, or significance test is licensed
by pretending the deterministic ranking is a randomized design.

If a sampled PR becomes inaccessible, has an unreconstructable history, or is
later found ineligible, keep its ID, rank, reason, and unknown/excluded status
in the sample accounting. Do not refill that slot after reveal. A mistaken
eligibility decision must be corrected transparently even if it removes a
positive. Any unauthorized inspection is logged, never silently erased.

## 5. Temporal boundary and allowed evidence

For each sampled PR define:

- `t_candidate`: earliest demonstrable time a public repair candidate snapshot
  was available in the PR. A commit's author/committer timestamp alone does
  not prove publication to the PR.
- `t_review`: earliest non-author candidate-evaluating review interaction,
  irrespective of whether it mentions a downstream obligation. Use submitted
  formal reviews (including approval-only), inline review comments, or PR
  discussion assessing code/behavior/tests. An automated review comment counts;
  a raw CI check does not. Earliest published comment time governs, not draft
  review creation time. For comments held in a pending review, use the review
  submission/publication event rather than the earlier draft-comment creation
  time; unavailable publication ordering is ambiguous. The custodian records
  excluded earlier events/reasons.
- `t0 = t_review^-`: the instant immediately before that first interaction.
  Store the cutoff timestamp as `t_review` with `t0_boundary=exclusive`, never
  invent a fractional instant before it. `pre_review_commit_sha` is the latest
  candidate state demonstrably published strictly before it; also retain the
  first candidate SHA when recoverable.
- `t_merge`: merge timestamp; review window is `[t_review, t_merge)`.
- `t_end = t_merge + 30 days`: fixed end of archival follow-up. Events in
  `[t_merge, t_end]` are post-review and cannot support primary O1/O2.

Require `t_candidate < t_review` for a positive temporal claim. An immediately
reviewed candidate, tied timestamps without proven ordering, a deleted first
review, or an unverifiable force-push sequence makes the relevant boundary
ambiguous. Do not use Git commit dates or today's PR diff as substitutes for
historical publication. Record timestamp precision and ordering evidence.
If review began before the candidate demonstrably existed, retain the sampled
case as temporally unassessable rather than redefining `t0` to a later comment.

### Phase A evidence packet

The custodian reconstructs a closed packet, with a manifest of included and
missing sources. It may contain only demonstrably pre-`t0` versions of:

- the initial PR title/body and subsequent edits known to predate `t0`;
- issues directly linked from those pre-review fields, their pre-`t0` bodies
  and comments, and documents explicitly linked by those issue bodies;
- the candidate code/diff and tests, with pinned head and base SHAs;
- repository specifications/conventions/docs at the pre-review base snapshot:
  root files matching `README*`, `CONTRIBUTING*`, `DEVELOPING*`, `HACKING*`,
  or `AGENTS*` (case-insensitive), and files matching those patterns in the
  nearest ancestor directory of each changed production file that contains
  such guidance; also docs/tests explicitly referenced by the pre-review PR,
  issues, or affected code;
- CI output and tool reports published strictly before `t0`.

The two Phase A coders may search the pinned pre-review source/test/doc tree
with queries derived from identifiers, components, and objective already in
the packet. Log queries and inspected paths. Each gets at most 120 minutes of
active inspection per PR, including searches. Infrastructure/retrieval delays
are logged separately. A timeout is a recorded coverage limitation, never
evidence of absence. Freeze the union of their independently inspected sources
as the permitted pre-review corpus. Neither coder receives the other's coding
before freezing their own record. Broad category predictions alone do not
establish recoverability of a specific proposition.

Do not follow links introduced during or after review into Phase A, even if
the target artifact is older. Such material may be recorded in Phase B as
`older_evidence_outside_packet`; it narrows an absence claim but cannot be
retroactively added to a blinded record. If that material demonstrably stated
the same obligation publicly before `t0`, mark `outside_packet_prior_explicit=true`
and disqualify primary O1, while retaining the original packet-relative coding.
This conservative contrary-evidence rule prevents a bounded packet from
manufacturing apparent emergence. It does not license adding post-review clues
to strengthen Phase A predictions. The corpus rule and inspection budget bound
all claims about non-explicitness or recoverability.

The packet excludes later review comments, later commits or tests, later PR
or issue edits, merge discussion, final implementation, post-merge material,
and retrospective explanations. If a current body has an edit after `t0` and
no trustworthy earlier version exists, mark that field unavailable. Stable
source IDs, retrieval time, and a URL alone do not establish historical text.
Use authenticated historical snapshots/edit versions where available; record
provenance and confidence. `created_at` with a later `updated_at` is insufficient
to reconstruct text. Missing linked issues, tests, logs, and documents remain
explicit missingness; no guessed reconstruction.

## 6. Blinded coding, freezing, and reveal

**Roles:** custodian, two independent coders, and an adjudicator. A custodian
or investigator already exposed to later histories cannot act as a blinded
coder for those cases. Both coders should be competent to read the language
and repository concepts; record expertise and prior project familiarity.
Neither participates in the sampled PRs. Calibration uses only invented
vignettes, never known pilots or confirmatory PRs. No new real examples are
collected to refine v1. AI assistance, if used for retrieval, is logged; a
shared AI transcript or the same model session is not independent coding.
Coders must not browse the live PR or communicate case judgments before freeze.

**Phase A — independent pre-review reconstruction.** Each coder receives only
the packet and pinned snapshots. They record apparent repair objective;
specific identifiable acceptance propositions and categories; explicit versus
inferred basis; inference steps; uncertainty and missingness; paths/queries;
and evidence pointers. “I would expect compatibility” is only a prediction,
not evidence of a specific compatibility obligation.

Serialize each record to UTF-8 JSON using sorted keys, no insignificant
whitespace, `ensure_ascii=false`, and a final LF; hash the exact bytes with
SHA-256. Hash every evidence file and the packet manifest. Commit the records
and packet manifests to a restricted staging repository or append-only
artifact store with external timestamped receipt; record its commit/receipt
and access permissions. A timestamp typed into a JSON file or an amendable
local commit alone is not a convincing ordering guarantee.

**All sampled Phase A records from both coders must be frozen and their
receipts recorded before either coder sees any sampled Phase B packet.** This
also limits learning from an early positive changing later pre-review coding.
If a record cannot be completed, freeze the missingness record instead. Do not
rewrite Phase A after reveal; corrections are separately hashed addenda marked
post-reveal and cannot strengthen the original blind predictions.

**Phase B — review reveal.** Release review/comment events, subsequent commits
and revised tests, the final merged state, and the fixed post-merge follow-up,
with provenance. Historical-text rules apply to Phase B too: an edited comment
with no recoverable in-window version cannot prove what was said at its creation
time. Each coder independently enumerates the union of Phase A acceptance
propositions and all candidate semantic obligations surfaced in the revealed
window, including rejected/disputed requests and negative evidence, rather
than selecting the strongest comment. Map propositions across repeated
comments; split a comment only when acceptance constraints are independently
violable. Record proposition-level temporal, recoverability, source, disposition,
and revision fields below. Cite both supporting and contrary spans.

In Phase B, both coders may examine the already frozen union of permitted
pre-review sources for the exact obligation. This is a **retrospective evidence
comparison**, separate from their unmodified Phase A predictions. Report both;
never present a retrospectively noticed inference as a blind prediction.
Subsequent evidence cannot enlarge the primary pre-review corpus.

**Phase C — reconciliation and adjudication.** Freeze both independent Phase B
records before discussion. A neutral coordinator aligns duplicate obligation
IDs using proposition and source-event identity without resolving labels;
each coder labels unmatched propositions as absent/undetected or an actual
classification in a separate reconciliation record. Preserve their original
lists to expose disagreement in obligation detection. Calculate agreement on
original PR-level outcomes and on the aligned obligation units, including
undetected units. The adjudicator reviews evidence and both rationales, records
a reasoned decision and dissent, and leaves irreducible ambiguity unresolved.
The primary researcher cannot silently settle disagreements toward O1/O2.

## 7. Independent coding dimensions

Use these categorical fields for each identified proposition. Values are not
collapsed into one “late contract” judgment. `none` where appropriate is an
observed negative; unavailable/ambiguous information is not.

| Field | Values and operational rules |
|---|---|
| `first_explicit_obligation_time` | `pre_review`: an unambiguous statement or direct executable assertion of the specific acceptance property exists in the permitted pre-`t0` corpus. `during_review`: first demonstrable explicit statement is in `[t_review,t_merge)` and the pre-review corpus supports no earlier explicit statement. `post_review`: first demonstrable statement is in `[t_merge,t_end]`; excluded from O1. `none`: no qualifying proposition is found in the complete observation window (PR-level empty-obligation summary). `ambiguous`: chronology, earlier text, or proposition identity is insufficient to distinguish these states. |
| `pre_review_recoverability` | `explicit`: the specific property is directly stated/asserted before `t0`. `strongly_inferable`: not explicit, but cited pre-review premises entail it by a short, documented chain using ordinary language/framework semantics, with no unstated domain-policy premise. `weakly_inferable`: clues exist but require an undocumented convention, policy choice, or speculative premise. `absent`: no supporting clue found within the frozen corpus after the recorded searches and with no material missingness. `unavailable`: essential historical material or inspection coverage is missing. `ambiguous`: available evidence permits materially different explicit/inference classifications. |
| `obligation_source` | `human_reviewer`: non-author account with supported human identity surfaces it. `automated_reviewer`: an identified review bot/tool comment surfaces it. `CI_or_tool`: first explicit event is a check/log/tool diagnostic rather than a review account. `author_self_revision`: author surfaces it without an earlier recorded external articulation. `linked_external_discussion`: it first appears in a temporally valid external discussion. `mixed`: inseparable co-origins, not merely later repetition. `other`: specify, including unknown actor identity. |
| `revision_effect` | `none`: complete observed history shows no implementation/test change responding to the obligation. `cosmetic`: only excluded presentation/admin effects. `local_adjustment`: semantic change stays within the existing mechanism, enforcement boundary, protected execution scope, and architectural responsibility. `strategy_change`: Section 8 is satisfied. `ambiguous`: missing diffs, conflicting interpretation, or unclear linkage prevents classification. |

`pre_review_recoverability=explicit` must agree with
`first_explicit_obligation_time=pre_review`; inconsistent records require
adjudication. A strongly inferable obligation can still be first explicit
during review. Never translate `absent` to “impossible for a competent engineer
to infer.” An ordinary repository convention only counts as explicit if its
pre-review text and applicability to this repair are demonstrated.

Record exact first-event ID/time, account type (`human`, `bot`, `unknown`), and
any disclosed AI assistance (`yes`, `no_disclosure`, `unknown`) separately.
A human account is not proof of exclusively human authorship. If the author
repeats a preceding bot request, preserve the bot as first source. If CI
articulates a property before the first review, code it pre-review. CI or author
articulation after `t0` may support O1 but must be reported separately from
reviewer-origin cases; this protocol uses “review-time” as a temporal term.

At PR level, an empty complete history uses `none`; do not fabricate an
obligation object merely to fill the schema. Record non-contract requests in
a separate request/exclusion list so style-heavy negatives remain auditable.

## 8. Material strategy change and evidential linkage

A strategy change requires an identified before/after difference in **how or
where the repair establishes an acceptance property**, supported by pinned
commit/diff and test evidence. At least one of these must be demonstrated:

- enforcement moves to, or adds, a distinct system boundary;
- one safety mechanism is replaced by a different mechanism class;
- a previously absent resource/work budget is introduced;
- handling is added for another version, node, or component;
- the class of executions protected by the repair changes;
- architectural responsibility moves between components;
- local validation becomes a cross-component solution.

State the old mechanism/boundary/scope, new mechanism/boundary/scope, applicable
indicator, and behavior making it material. A changed constant within an
existing budget, renamed function, added comment, equivalent refactor, routine
assertion, or mechanical correction alone is not enough. Lines changed and
number of commits are not proxies. A one-line change can be material only if
the semantic evidence demonstrates one of the listed changes; a large diff
can still be local or cosmetic. Unclear cases remain ambiguous.

For primary O2, require all of: an O1-positive obligation; the candidate
snapshot before its articulation; a subsequent strategy-changing revision
before merge; and an explicit public response/commit explanation connecting
that revision to this obligation. The before/after diff must substantiate that
connection. Record concurrent requests and competing explanations. Temporal
succession or plausible semantic fit alone is `linkage=temporal_only` or
`semantic_fit_only`, not primary O2. Conflicting drivers or an already planned
change make linkage ambiguous unless the public record distinguishes them.
This conservative linkage rule can miss real consequences; report that limit.

Record `linkage` as `explicit_supported`, `semantic_fit_only`, `temporal_only`,
`contradicted`, `none`, or `ambiguous`. Preserve strategy changes unrelated to
new obligations as negative evidence for the proposed O1-to-O2 framing. Review
comments and author explanations remain observational evidence, not proof that
no alternative cause operated.

## 9. Outcome rules and analysis frozen in advance

### Primary obligation-level outcomes

**O1 positive** requires every condition below:

1. `contract_qualification=yes`, with all four operational tests supported;
2. a demonstrable candidate predates the first review;
3. first explicit time is `during_review`, with reconstructable chronology;
4. recoverability is `strongly_inferable`, `weakly_inferable`, or `absent`,
   never `explicit`, `unavailable`, or `ambiguous`;
5. disposition is `accepted`, supported as in Section 3;
6. no demonstrable earlier public explicit statement has been found outside
   the packet (`outside_packet_prior_explicit=false`); an unresolved chronology
   for a relevant external counterexample makes the outcome unknown;
7. the blinded-record, evidence-integrity, and independent-coding procedures
   were followed, with a resolved adjudication supporting these conditions.

**O2 positive** additionally requires `revision_effect=strategy_change` and
`linkage=explicit_supported` under Section 8. Consequently O2 implies O1
within this codebook. A changed test alone does not prove changed strategy.
Before adjudication, coders independently assign provisional O1/O2 labels
using the substantive conditions; condition 7 is the final procedural gate.
Those original provisional labels, not the adjudicated ones, enter agreement
calculations.

For each outcome use `positive`, `negative`, or `unknown`. A decisive observed
failure of a necessary condition can establish a negative; missing evidence
that could change the decision yields unknown. Disputed or unresolved
obligations are unknown for acceptance; explicitly retracted non-requirements
are negative. Report the failed condition or unresolved field, not only the
final label. Lack of change is negative O2 only when the post-articulation
history is complete enough to establish it.

### PR aggregation and reporting

A PR is positive for O1/O2 if at least one adjudicated obligation qualifies.
It is negative only if the complete permitted history was assessed, none
qualifies, and no unresolved obligation could change that outcome. Otherwise
it is unknown. A positive can coexist with other unknown obligations; flag
incomplete coverage. Count each PR once per outcome. Report obligation counts
separately without treating them as independent PR observations.

Report the complete flow from candidate frame through screening, sample,
exclusions, blinding failures, temporal failures, and outcomes. For each O1/O2
report `P`, `N`, and `U` among the `S` selected slots; keep confirmed ineligible
slots in a separately identified `X` group, so `S=P+N+U+X`. Show `P/S` as the
observed positive fraction of selected slots, not a prevalence estimate.
Also show `P/(P+N)` with its ascertainment limitation, and conservative
missingness bounds `[P/(S-X), (P+U)/(S-X)]` for the remaining eligible or
unresolved selected slots as an accounting sensitivity check, not confidence
intervals. Confirmed ineligible slots are not negatives or hypothetical
positives. If a denominator is zero, report `not estimable`.
A complete eligible-frame census still cannot generalize past that frame.

Cross-tabulate first explicit time against pre-review recoverability; report
Phase A prediction coverage separately. Tabulate source, disposition, revision
effect, linkage, and unknown/exclusion reasons. Report O2 among O1 positives
and all selected PRs, with denominators. Display counts for human, automated,
CI/tool, author, and uncertain sources, without claiming causal differences.
Accepted semantic obligations used in framing summaries include pre-review
propositions from Phase A as well as review-time propositions; do not calculate
pre-review rates from a list limited to newly surfaced requests. Report disputed,
retracted, and unresolved units outside that accepted-obligation denominator.
No confirmatory subgroup significance tests or outcome-driven taxonomy merging
are permitted. New subgroup questions are exploratory and labelled as such.
No effect size or frequency is assumed in advance. A positive case can support
bounded occurrence, never a claim that the phenomenon is typical.

## 10. Independent coding and agreement gates

Every sampled PR receives two independent Phase A and Phase B codings,
including all apparent positives, negatives, and unknowns. This avoids
estimating agreement only on an outcome-selected subset. Coders cannot share
judgments until both stages are frozen as specified. Preserve original labels,
obligation-detection differences, adjudicated labels, and rationale.

Before adjudication, report raw agreement, contingency tables, and unweighted
Cohen's kappa for the two coders on PR-level O1 and O2 and on each categorical
obligation field after unit alignment. Treat categories as nominal, including
unknown/ambiguous; the recoverability list is not an ordinal scale throughout.
For multi-label categories, report per-category binary agreement and label-set
agreement. Include unmatched detections as `undetected` in field tables and
report their count, rather than quietly dropping them.

The predeclared interpretability gate for each primary PR outcome and each
critical field (qualification, first explicit time, recoverability, disposition,
revision effect, linkage) is at least 20 paired units, raw agreement at least
0.80, and kappa at least 0.60 **where estimable**. These are pragmatic study
gates, not universal validity standards. Report uncertainty and class imbalance
rather than treating a cutoff as proof of correctness. If kappa is undefined
because of invariant labels, report `not estimable`; do not replace it with
1.0 or silently switch metrics. Sparse or invariant fields leave reliability
for that distinction unestablished, even with high raw agreement.

If a critical gate fails or is unassessable, publish the counts and disagreements
as a feasibility/measurement result; do not present an aggregate O1/O2 finding
as a reliably validated classification. Verified illustrative observations and
null counts can still be described with this limitation. Revise the codebook
only in v2, preserving v1 results and using fresh data for renewed confirmation.
Adjudicated agreement is not inter-rater reliability. No reliability is claimed
until measured, and adjudication cannot erase a failed gate.

## 11. Negative evidence and stopping/kill criteria

The following dispositions are fixed before observation. Thresholds below are
transparent framing/feasibility decisions, not significance tests or estimates
of universal importance.

| Observation | Required response |
|---|---|
| No primary O1 positives | Report that v1 did not observe accepted review-time non-explicit obligations in its assessable sample. Do not search for replacements; if unknowns are large, call it inconclusive rather than evidence of absence. |
| O1 occurs but no primary O2 qualifies | Drop the material-strategy-consequence claim for v1; report local/cosmetic/unlinked changes and unknowns. |
| At least 90% of temporally assessable accepted semantic obligations are explicit before review | Treat the “usually emerges during review” framing as unsupported in this frame; that framing was never a licensed v1 claim. Report the actual denominator, including small counts. |
| At least 90% of O1 obligations are strongly inferable from the permitted pre-review corpus | Reject any stronger framing of O1 as evidence that the relevant contract was not reasonably recoverable beforehand; retain only non-explicitness if supported. |
| At least 90% of review requests fail the semantic-contract test or yield only cosmetic/admin effects | Report limited yield of the construct in this frame; do not broaden “contract” to raise the count. |
| Material strategy changes have only temporal linkage, conflicting drivers, or unrelated explicit causes | Do not count them as primary O2; weaken the obligation-to-revision framing. |
| More than 20% of selected slots lack a usable temporal boundary or material historical evidence | Treat temporal feasibility as failed; report missingness bounds and reasons, not a validated frame-level occurrence characterization. |
| Independent-coding gate fails or cannot be assessed | Apply Section 10; do not repair reliability by selecting agreeable cases. |
| Enumeration is incomplete, exclusions encode outcomes, or the frame contains no review-observable repairs | Stop confirmatory execution, publish the failure and audit trail, and require a new pre-data protocol/frame for another attempt. |

Zero denominators are `not estimable`, not passed gates. Review-request
proportions use all coded candidate-evaluating request units, with repeated
requests for the same change deduplicated by source/thread and proposition;
report how many have ambiguous qualification. The 90% summaries are descriptive
framing checks, not empirical thresholds defining a positive case. Unknown
cases never become negatives to reach a threshold.

A null or negative finding is a valid, reportable study outcome. It can falsify
or sharply narrow the motivating story without affecting the frozen M3 theory.
No publication acceptance is promised. Unfavorable results, exclusions, and
coding disagreements must remain in the report and evidence accounting.

## 12. Relation to M3 and bounded Cross-Contract Loss

The motivation overlaps with M3's broader question: what conclusions or
reports are licensed by a machine-validated repair artifact under a particular
contract? This protocol investigates a possible practical antecedent: a
relevant downstream obligation may not be explicit in the available evidence
when candidate generation begins. Whether it is inferable remains a distinct
empirical question. The study observes public candidate availability, not
private generation start time or an engineer's knowledge state.

The empirical construct does not automatically instantiate a Lean theorem,
establish M3's assumptions, validate M3, or add evidence for Candidate B.
The current integrated-paper theory remains governed by
[`docs/m3_current_paper_freeze.md`](../docs/m3_current_paper_freeze.md).
M3-A/B/C, the integrated manuscript, M2, M4, and XAI are outside this task.
The protocol does not revise research-priority or publication decisions.

The possible research chain consists of three separate evidence streams:

```text
Public review histories -> O1/O2: scoped articulation and revision observations
Separate bounded-search experiment -> F1: controlled candidate-loss mechanism
Future production-workflow study -> F2: direct causal evidence, if obtainable
```

None of these arrows supplies another stream's missing evidence. In particular,
O1/O2 do not show a fixed generation budget, a pre-bound search contract,
excluded candidates, alternate-contract validity, or the counterfactual repair
frontier. **F2 = NOT CLAIMED**, regardless of how many O1/O2 cases are found.
No Cross-Contract Loss experiment is implemented or executed by this protocol.

## 13. Threats, mitigations, and residual limits

| Threat | Mitigation | Residual limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Construct validity | Proposition/consequence/scope tests, accepted disposition, strict material-diff and linkage rules. | Public language may not reflect actual acceptance authority; conservative rules may miss real obligations. |
| Selection and known-positive bias | Complete candidate listing, fixed dates/ranking, pilot/derivative quarantine, no replacement after reveal. | Project choice was hypothesis-informed; eligibility/availability select a conditional frame. |
| Hindsight bias | Two blind Phase A records frozen for the full sample before any reveal; later inference comparison labelled retrospective. | Domain expertise and prior non-case-specific knowledge cannot be removed. |
| Survivorship/merge bias | State merged-only population and count screening exclusions. | Rejected/abandoned repairs and obligations preventing merge are not represented. |
| Repository conventions | Pin accessible convention docs and record inference premises/searches. | Tacit conventions may make an apparently absent obligation obvious to maintainers. |
| Reviewer expertise | Record observable role/familiarity and source without ranking participants. | Expertise and authority cannot reliably be inferred from public roles. |
| AI/bot effects | Separate source account type, tool diagnostics, disclosed assistance, and uncertain provenance. | Undisclosed AI use behind a human account remains unknown. |
| Incomplete edits/force-pushes | Historical publication evidence, immutable SHAs, explicit unavailable fields and temporal unknowns. | Deleted reviews, issues, diffs, and expiring CI logs may prevent reconstruction. |
| Mutable PR/issue descriptions | Require valid pre-`t0` versions; current text is not automatically historical. | Timestamped API retrieval does not authenticate what the text said earlier. |
| Hidden organizational context | Bound claims to public corpus; record external references and disputes. | Offline discussion may precede public articulation and explain revisions. |
| Coder subjectivity and unitization | Full independent double coding, unmatched-detection accounting, neutral adjudication, agreement gates. | Shared training and background can produce correlated errors. |
| Single ecosystem | State one repository and quarter in every aggregate claim. | Findings do not transfer automatically to other languages, organizations, APR, or formal verification. |
| Temporal review-practice changes | Fixed quarter and observation window; freeze tool versions/retrieval metadata. | Later bot adoption, review norms, or workflow changes can alter applicability. |
| Corpus/inspection-budget limits | Fixed source rule and timed/logged inspection; missingness cannot prove absence. | “Absent” is search-bounded, not an exhaustive statement about all available knowledge. |
| Observational attribution | Require explicit linkage plus diffs and record competing drivers. | Author explanations and temporal order cannot identify a causal effect of pre-binding. |

Collect only public evidence needed for coding; avoid unnecessary personal
identifiers and incidental sensitive contents. Stable source IDs and bounded
excerpts plus hashes are preferred to indiscriminate redistribution. Record
redactions/licensing or access limits and how they restrict independent audit;
do not claim publicly reproducible text when the evidence cannot be shared.

Execution order for a future authorized study:

   until that identity is recorded. Name custodian, two coders, and adjudicator;

## 16. Internal adversarial audit at protocol freeze

This is an authoring self-audit, not independent peer review or measured
inter-rater reliability. The controls below are methodological commitments;
their execution must be audited later.

| Challenge | Resolution or acknowledged limit |
|---|---|
| Could cases be cherry-picked? | Fixed calendar frame, exhaustive enumeration, independent pre-outcome screening, fixed ranking/cap, full exclusion ledger, no post-reveal replacement (Section 4). Project choice remains hypothesis-informed. |
| Could rules change after outcomes? | Immutable v1, remote freeze identity, versioned amendments, and full-sample Phase A freeze (Sections 6 and 15). Deviations cannot retain an unqualified confirmatory label. |
| Is “contract” operational? | Required proposition, violated consequence, downstream scope, evidence and acceptance disposition; non-semantic requests excluded (Section 3). |
| Is non-explicit equated with non-inferable? | Separate timing/recoverability dimensions and separate blind prediction/retrospective comparison; no impossible-to-infer claim (Sections 6–7). |
| Does review occurrence become a pre-binding causal claim? | O1/O2/F1/F2 ladder, strict observational linkage, F2 always NOT CLAIMED (Sections 1, 8, and 12). |
| Do pilots contaminate confirmation? | IDs/derivatives quarantined; no calibration/counts/agreement use; coder-exposure declarations and replacements (Section 2). |
| Can cosmetics count as strategy-changing contracts? | Semantic qualification plus material mechanism/boundary/scope diff plus explicit linkage all required (Sections 3 and 8–9). |
| Can another coder reproduce key decisions? | Defined categories, fixed packets/search logs, full double coding, unmatched-unit accounting, original labels and adjudication evidence retained (Sections 5–10). Tacit knowledge remains a limit. |
| Are AI/bot comments distinguished? | Separate source/account/disclosed-assistance fields, and separate CI events (Section 7). Hidden assistance remains unknown. |
| Would a null be honestly reported? | No outcome-driven stopping or expansion; negative/unknown accounting, explicit kill gates and publication of measurement failures (Sections 9–11). Venue acceptance is not promised. |
